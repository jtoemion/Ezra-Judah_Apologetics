# Daniel 7:13-14 — "One Like a Son of Man" and the Claims of Jesus

## Core Argument (Sam Shamoun)

### The Daniel7 Vision

Daniel 7 describes a four-beast sequence (lion, bear, leopard, unnamed beast) representing successive earthly kingdoms. Then:

> "I saw in the night visions, and behold, with the clouds of heaven there came one like a son of man, and he came to the Ancient of Days and was presented before him. And to him was given dominion and glory and a kingdom, that all peoples, nations, and languages should serve him; his dominion is an everlasting dominion which shall not pass away, and his kingdom one that shall not be destroyed." — Daniel 7:13-14

### The Argument Structure

**1. "Son of Man" Here Is Not Merely Human**

The title "son of man" (Hebrew: *bar enash*) in this specific context is not a generic human reference. Compare:
- Elsewhere in Daniel, "son of man" is used as a straightforward human designation (e.g., Daniel 8:17 where Gabriel addresses Daniel as "son of man" simply = human)
- But in Daniel 7:13-14, this figure is described as **coming with the clouds of heaven** — language that in biblical cosmology is exclusively divine (cf. Psalm 104:3 — God who "makes the clouds his chariot")

**2. Clouds of Heaven = Divine Transport**

The phrase "coming with the clouds of heaven" is reserved for God alone in Old Testament theophany. When is the only other figure described this way? When God descends in a cloud. This is not how ordinary humans are described in Scripture.

**3. This Figure Receives an Everlasting Kingdom from the Ancient of Days**

The one like a son of man does not claim the kingdom for himself — he *receives* it from the Ancient of Days (God). This is given authority, not inherited through conquest. The kingdom is:
- Everlasting (no end)
- Universal (all peoples serve him)
- Indestructible

This matches nothing in human experience.

**4. Jesus Explicitly Applies Daniel 7:13-14 to Himself**

In the Gospel accounts, Jesus unambiguously identifies himself with this "son of man" figure:
- Matthew 26:64 / Luke 22:69: "Hereafter you will see the Son of Man seated at the right hand of Power, and coming on the clouds of heaven" — Jesus quotes Daniel 7:13 directly
- Mark 14:61-62: Same self-identification
- Matthew 28:18: "All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me" — echoes the given dominion of Daniel 7:14

**5. The Islamic Counter (and Response)**

Islam argues "son of man" in Daniel7 means "humanity in general" and cannot be a messianic prophecy. However:
- The figure in Daniel 7 receives a kingdom, not a collective group of humans
- Daniel 7:27: "The kingdom and the dominion... shall be given to the people of the saints of the Most High" — the saints receive the kingdom *from* the son of man, not as the son of man themselves
- The early Jewish targum (Targum Jonathan on Isaiah 52:13) explicitly calls the suffering servant "My servant the Messiah" — Jewish scholarship itself recognized a messianic individual in these texts

### The "Islamic Rebuttal" Summary

| Islamic Objection | Christian Response |
|---|---|
| "Son of man" = generic human (bar enosh) | Daniel 7:13-14 does not describe a generic human — it describes a unique individual receiving cosmic dominion from God |
| Clouds of heaven = symbolic, not divine | Clouds are theophanic transport throughout Scripture; used for God alone |
| The kingdom is given to Israel's saints collectively | Daniel 7:27 says saints receive kingdom *from* the son of man — he is distinct from them |
| Daniel's "son of man" is just Daniel himself | Daniel is the human recipient in other visions (Daniel 8:17); in chapter 7, the one like a son of man is presented *to* the Ancient of Days, not Daniel |
